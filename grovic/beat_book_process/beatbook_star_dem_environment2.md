## 3) Aquaculture Deep Dive
Aquaculture on the Eastern Shore includes oyster, clam, mussel, and seaweed farming, as well as hatcheries and leases. The regulatory framework involves multiple agencies:
* Maryland Department of Natural Resources: leasing and fisheries
* Maryland Department of the Environment: permits and discharges
* U.S. Army Corps: permits
* Local planning and zoning boards: land use and zoning
* NOAA: science and policy context

Common friction points include siting, navigation, habitat, water quality, and working waterfront conflicts. To watch next:
* Trends in aquaculture technology and financing
* Legal challenges to aquaculture permits and regulations
* Industry developments, such as new species or products

To cover a new aquaculture proposal, consider the following checklist:
1. Identify the proposal and the company/individual behind it
2. Determine the location and size of the proposed aquaculture operation
3. Research the regulatory framework and permitting process
4. Interview local watermen, residents, and business owners
5. Review environmental impact assessments and other relevant documents
6. Follow up on any changes or updates to the proposal
