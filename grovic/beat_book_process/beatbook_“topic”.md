2. Workforce development: The aquaculture industry is in need of skilled workers, and there is a focus on developing training programs and apprenticeships.
3. Economic development: The aquaculture industry has the potential to contribute to the local economy, but needs support and investment.

**Important Agencies:**

1. USDA (United States Department of Agriculture): Providing grant funding for blue catfish processors and purchasing blue catfish for low-income areas.
2. Maryland Department of Agriculture: Partnering with the USDA to purchase blue catfish and support the aquaculture industry.
3. Department of Labor: Focusing on workforce development and providing funding for training programs and apprenticeships.

**Key People/Stakeholders:**

1. Nick Hargrove, owner of Tilghman Island Seafood: A blue catfish processor who has benefited from USDA grant funding.
2. Brooke Rollins, U.S. Secretary of Agriculture: Announcing grant funding for blue catfish processors and purchasing blue catfish for low-income areas.
3. Andy Harris, Rep. (R-Md., 1st): Supporting the development of the commercial market for blue catfish.
4. Lori Chavez-DeRemer, U.S. Secretary of Labor: Focusing on workforce development and visiting the Horn Point Oyster Hatchery to learn about the aquaculture industry.
5. Stephanie Alexander, Manager of the UMCES Horn Point Laboratory: Hosting the U.S. Secretary of Labor and discussing workforce development and the aquaculture industry.

**Trends/Conflicts:**

1. Growing interest in aquaculture: The USDA and Department of Labor are investing in the industry, and there is a focus on developing training programs and apprenticeships.
2. Conflict between economic development and environmental concerns: The blue catfish is an invasive species that is causing environmental harm, but the industry is also seen as a potential economic opportunity.
3. Federal budget cuts: The Department of Labor is working to prevent waste, fraud, and abuse, and is streamlining operations to modernize the agency.

**Relevant to Covering this Beat:**

1. Monitor USDA and Department of Labor initiatives: Track grant funding, purchasing programs, and workforce development initiatives.
2. Follow the development of the aquaculture industry: Watch for new businesses, investments, and innovations in the industry.
3. Investigate the impact of invasive species: Examine the environmental and economic impacts of the blue catfish and other invasive species in the Chesapeake Bay.
